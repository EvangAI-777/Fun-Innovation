Engineering Over Fear
Scarcity is a distribution problem, not a planetary one.

This simulation shows how populations self-regulate, crash, and recover through systemic dynamics — not absolute limits. The real world works the same way. Every "limit" below is a tunable parameter — in the sim and on Earth.

1. The Space Factor
8.2B in Texas At NYC density (~27k/sq mi), all 8.2 billion humans fit inside the borders of Texas.
55% of US land At comfortable suburban density (houses and yards), all 8.2B fit in the US using 55% of the land — leaving 45% as pristine wilderness.
1 acre per family Every family of 4 on Earth gets a 1-acre homestead within US borders, with 400 million acres left over for national parks.
Sim Connection
The maxEntities caps in this simulation look like hard limits — but increase plant growth rate and the whole food chain stabilizes. The constraint isn't space. It's resource flow.
2. The Energy Spine
1 Texas of solar A solar farm the size of Texas generates 100% of the energy needed for 8.2 billion people living a high-level American lifestyle.
~11,600 reactors Modern nuclear reactors occupying less than 1% of US land provide complete carbon-free, constant power for the entire species.
Sim Connection
Plant growth rate is the energy backbone of this ecosystem. Crank it up and the whole food chain stabilizes. The sun delivers 10,000x what civilization uses — the bottleneck is capture, not supply.
3. The Water Myth
1T+ gal/day wasted The US alone discards over 1 trillion gallons of freshwater into the ocean every day via the Mississippi, Columbia, and other major rivers.
672B gal/day needed Total global domestic water need at US standards is ~672 billion gallons/day. We naturally let double that amount flow into the sea unused.
Sim Connection
When plants drop below 5, this sim seeds new ones automatically — that's not magic, it's engineering a recovery pathway. Desalination, capture, and distribution are the real-world equivalent. The water exists; the pipes don't.
4. The Waste Loop
14,000 °C Plasma gasification at 14,000 °C converts any waste stream into clean syngas (energy) and inert slag (construction material). Zero landfill.
Misplaced resources Trash is just misplaced resources. Every output becomes an input in a closed-loop system — moving from panic to engineering solves the loop.
Sim Connection
Dead entities in the simulation disappear — energy lost forever. A real ecosystem recycles everything. Plasma gasification closes the loop for civilization the same way decomposition does for biology.
Bottom Line
If we can fit everyone in one country with a yard, power them with one state's sun, and turn their trash into energy — overpopulation is a choice, not a fate. We have a broken system, not a broken planet.

Every "limit" in this sim is a tunable parameter.
So is every real-world constraint — if you engineer it.
